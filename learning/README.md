# Learning route

The 30 project-defined milestones connect a two-qubit teleportation experiment to the thermal resource's useful-cooling law and rare-spin rate. The route uses one educational anchor: **John Watrous, _The Theory of Quantum Information_ (2018)**.

| Route | Purpose |
|---|---|
| [Anchor and bridge](anchor-and-bridge.md) | Relevant textbook sections, notation conventions and a complete two-spin example with every outcome counted |
| [30 milestones](milestones.md) | The background and narrative dependencies needed to understand the contribution as a whole |
| [Scientific account](../science/README.md) | Canonical models, statements and proofs |
| [Interpretation](../science/interpretation.md) | Physical meaning, finite comparison and resource boundaries |
| [Verification](../verification/README.md) | The scope of reproducible finite checks |

Begin with the bridge's two-spin example, then follow the milestone dependencies into the scientific files. The textbook supplies channels, fidelity, LOCC, partial transposition, teleportation and permutation language. The project supplies the collective-spin spectrum, Gibbs weights, receiver construction and asymptotic argument.

The distinction running through the route is between the presence of entanglement and a fixed unconditional transmission advantage. A rare successful event matters only with its actual probability, and quantum cooperation matters only in the laboratory partition where it is allowed.
